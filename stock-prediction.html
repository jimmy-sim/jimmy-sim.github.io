Comparing Neural Networks for Stock Price Forecasting

Jimmy Youngjae Sim · Project completed May–September 2025

This project explored how different neural network architectures model financial time series. Using Python and TensorFlow/Keras, I compared a vanilla recurrent neural network (RNN), a long short-term memory network (LSTM), and a conditional variational autoencoder (VAE) on five years of stock data.

Approach

I compared forecasts for Apple, Amazon, Alphabet, Netflix, and NVIDIA, varying the number of training epochs and batch size. The work was discussed with a mentor, using plots of observed and predicted prices to examine how the models behaved under different training settings.

The comparison focused on recurrent memory in the RNN and LSTM, and a latent-variable approach in the conditional VAE.

An illustrative comparison

These AAPL plots show one experimental setting from the project. The vanilla RNN produces a relatively flat forecast, while the LSTM follows more of the variation in the observed series. Both models still miss substantial price movements.

Red: observed prices. Black: model predictions. Original plots from the project; select either image to view it at full size.

Takeaways

The experiments helped me understand how architecture and training choices affect time-series forecasts. In this example, the LSTM follows the observed series more closely than the vanilla RNN, though the forecasts remain smoother than the actual prices.

These plots illustrate model behavior rather than a complete benchmark: they do not establish performance on unseen data or trading profitability. A reproducible comparison would require the original preprocessing, data splits, evaluation code, and baseline results.
